Current doctoral research direction

Laser measurement instrument development is my current doctoral research direction. I am developing the optical foundations and entering the group's instrument-development work, while the specific technical branch remains under active evaluation.

This is a living research page. It records current study, project entry and research questions; it does not imply that a laser instrument, optical diagnostic system or doctoral publication has already been completed.

Optical foundations for instrument development

  1. Physical optics

    Build the common language

    Wave optics, interference, diffraction, polarization and the relationship between optical fields and measurement signals.

  2. Laser physics

    Understand the light source

    Laser principles, resonators, gain and the behavior of a source in a measurement system.

  3. Nonlinear optics

    Understand frequency conversion

    Develop the concepts needed to approach parametric processes and OPO-related work.

  4. Measurement

    Connect optics to data

    Consider detector response, signal processing, calibration, uncertainty and the full diagnostic chain.

Reading & learning map

The current starting point is the seventh edition of Yao Qijun’s 《光学教程》. Further study connects physical optics with laser principles, nonlinear optics and optical measurement.

The sequence prioritizes foundations shared by the candidate directions. Reading progress and worked exercises can be added here without presenting planned study as completed expertise.

A practical learning loop

Read the physical argument, work through the mathematics, reproduce a simple calculation, then relate it to an actual instrument or diagnostic question.

Questions for entering the project

  • What physical quantity should the instrument measure, at what spatial and temporal scales?
  • What source, wavelength range and optical geometry does the measurement require?
  • Which part of the system is a research question rather than routine integration?
  • What calibration, uncertainty analysis and reference experiment will support the eventual claim?
  • How can the optics, electronics and acquisition chain be tested in stages?

Future experiments · planned

Future work may include optical alignment, source characterization, calibration and diagnostic experiments, depending on the selected branch and the group’s project needs.

These are current development objectives. Instrument specifications, experimental results and performance numbers will be added only when the corresponding test records exist.
