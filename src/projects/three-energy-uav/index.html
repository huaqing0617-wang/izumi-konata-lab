A mission that has to close

The course-design problem is a fixed-wing long-endurance UAV supplied by solar energy, a Li-ion battery and a hydrogen fuel cell. The nominal mission target is at least 24 hours; several simulation versions use a 24.5-hour mission window.

The core problem is not simply adding stored energy. Additional energy-system mass raises the required power, which in turn changes the required energy and mass. The project studies that loop together with energy management and hardware constraints.

Aircraft, power & mass closure

Aircraft and aerodynamic assumptions feed the flight-power requirement. Propulsion efficiencies and auxiliary loads determine the electrical demand; the source model then sizes the energy system and updates total mass.

Model layerRoleWhat must be checked
Mission & aircraftFlight condition and operating timelineMission window, mass, geometry and units.
Aerodynamics & powerTranslate flight conditions into demandDrag assumptions, propulsive efficiency and takeoff feasibility.
Source sizingSolar / Li-ion / hydrogen contributionIrradiance, usable capacity, fuel-cell behavior and storage mass.
Mass closureFeed energy-system mass back into powerExistence of a feasible solution under the stated assumptions.
Hardware limitsApply the available componentsMotor, battery, photovoltaic and controller limits.

A theoretical feasible design and the endurance of the current hardware are distinct questions. A mass-closure result must be interpreted within the assumptions that generated it.

Three sources, different roles

SourceModeled contributionEngineering constraint
SolarIrradiance-dependent power and possible battery chargingAvailable area, real panel output and conversion losses.
Li-ionStored energy and support during source shortfallsUsable capacity, voltage and allowed charge/discharge conditions.
Hydrogen fuel cellEnergy supply coupled to hydrogen storageFuel-cell capability and the mass of the storage system.

Single-, dual- and triple-source cases are compared with consistent mission and mass accounting. A two- or three-fold endurance advantage is not assumed as a target result.

Energy management, in simulation and hardware

The MATLAB / Simulink framework uses Stateflow to describe source coordination. EMS studies examine when solar power can serve the load or charge the battery, when the battery or fuel cell supplies power, and how source or voltage limits alter the operating decision.

The first hardware direction is deliberately simple: solar and fuel-cell paths feed the bus through their conversion stages, while the Li-ion path supplies the bus directly through its protection and switching branch. No bidirectional DC–DC stage is introduced.

  • Keep the voltage sensing and overvoltage charging cutoff explicit.
  • Record MOSFET switching, fusing and reverse-current protection against the actual schematic.
  • Cross-check simulation decisions and the hardware implementation instead of treating them as automatically equivalent.

What the parameter studies resolve

Earlier investigations compare drag-coefficient assumptions, hydrogen-storage mass fractions and geometry. Later studies examine how maximum endurance changes with mass, and distinguish an energy limit from a propulsion-power or takeoff limit.

A sweep that stops because the configured motor cannot satisfy takeoff does not by itself demonstrate an energy-only endurance boundary. The follow-up framing therefore separates unconstrained propulsion-power studies from the minimum power needed for takeoff at each mass.

Results & their limits

The project has produced successive simulation frameworks, feasibility investigations and an EMS hardware design. Some model versions fail the mission target; the archive retains these failures and the assumptions that lead to them.

  • Do not compare configurations using different statistical windows or hidden mass assumptions.
  • Check unmet load as well as final battery state and total source energy.
  • Keep predicted endurance separate from measured endurance.
  • Apply real photovoltaic and propulsion limits before making a current-hardware claim.

No 24-hour flight demonstration or guaranteed source-combination advantage is claimed on this page.
